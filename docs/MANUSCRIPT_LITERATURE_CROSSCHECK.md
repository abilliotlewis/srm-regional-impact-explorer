## Statements requiring literature follow-up

The current reference set does not adequately support the following claims. They should be omitted or explicitly marked for a targeted literature update before manuscript submission.

- “This is the first matched four-model comparison of G6solar and G6sulfur over the Southeast United States.” Establishing novelty requires a systematic search, not inference from the current seven papers.
- Claims about observed Southeast US heat, precipitation, drought, or vulnerability trends. The current bibliography is SRM-focused and lacks regional observational references.
- Formal methodological justification for the chosen five-year moving-block length, percentile interval, or `d_z` interpretation under independently evolving climate simulations. The code is transparent and sensitivity-tested, but the manuscript should add an appropriate statistical-method citation if the journal expects one.
- Claims that the four models span or represent total GeoMIP structural uncertainty. No current citation supports that sampling interpretation.
- Formal citations for the US Census boundary file, Natural Earth land product, and WGS84 geodesic convention should be formatted as dataset/software references according to the target journal.

## Introduction and Discussion guardrails

The Introduction can safely move from broad SRM context (Parson and Keith), to standardized G6 experiments (Kravitz; Visioni), to known method- and model-dependent regional responses (Visioni; Jones), and then to the particular difficulty of hydrologic inference (Ricke). The repository's numerical findings should first appear in Results, not be attributed to those references.

The Discussion can compare the observed temperature-versus-precipitation contrast with prior literature, but should avoid claiming direct replication when study regions, models, experiment families, periods, or resolutions differ. Feng is Southeast Asia, Dagon and Schrag use a different modeling framework, and Sun et al. is a convection-permitting case study rather than a G6 ensemble.
